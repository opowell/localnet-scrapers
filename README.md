# Localnet scrapers

The scraper source that Localnet, a browser extension that is a personal search engine, starts with. `source.json` lists
the repo of every scraper; Localnet reads what each one scrapes from its own `source.json`, and pulls a scraper's code only when it first
scrapes a page the scraper reads.

To add a scraper, give it a repo of its own (see any listed here) and add the repo's address to `sources`.

## What the scrapers read

What the scrapers read that several share; each scraper's own README may say more of its own.

Sites' own searches are read as listings of what they find too, so a search started from the site's home page (see Context and start item) goes on to them, the first most strongly: a **Reddit** search (`/search/?q=…`, or a subreddit's, `/r/rust/search/?q=…`) links to each post found as its comments page, titled by its link (a result's `a[data-testid="post-title"]`, old Reddit's `a.search-title`, or any link to a post's comments, which a comment found stands for), waited for up to 10 s (see `searchResultsOf`, so a search with no results fails); a **RedGIFs** search (`/search?query=…`) is read as a niche's feed is; a **GitHub** search for repositories (`/search?q=…&type=repositories`, `p=2` for the second page of ten) is an app drawn from the data the server writes into it (`react-app.embeddedData`'s `payload.blackbirdSearchRoute.results`), each result naming its repository's `owner_login` and `name`, linked as `/<owner>/<name>`, titled `"<owner>/<name>"`; Simon Willison's (`/search/?q=…`) links to each entry found (`a[rel="bookmark"]`), John D. Cook's (`/blog/?s=…`) to each post (`article.post .entry-title a`), as his author pages do, and Terence Tao's (`/?s=…`) to each post brief's link (`.post-brief a[rel="bookmark"]`). Two searches are on sites of their own, each with a scraper of its own: **Hacker News Search** (`hn.algolia.com/?q=…`, `content-scripts/parsers/hackernews-search.js`), an app that asks its API for the stories, so the parser asks it too, as the page would (`/api/v1/search?query=…&tags=story&page=<page>`, or `search_by_date` with `sort=byDate`; 20 a page), linking to each story's discussion (`https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=<objectID>`), titled by its title; and **Literotica Search** (`search.literotica.com/?query=…`, `content-scripts/parsers/literotica-search.js`), whose results are schema.org list items (`[property="itemListElement"]`), each naming its work as its `resource`, titled by its heading, which unlike the site's class names carry no build hash.

The news parsers link listing pages to what they list, so a search reaches each story from the page that lists it. A link's weight falls with the story's place on the page, `1 / (1 + rank / 10)` for rank 0, 1, 2, …, so the first story gets weight 1 and the 31st gets 0.25. On Hacker News the rank is the one the listing shows beside each story, which carries on across pages, so the first story on page 2 (`?p=2`, shown as 31) gets 0.25 rather than 1. Hacker News listings (the front page, newest, ask, show, …) link to each story's discussion page (`/item?id=…`), and a discussion page links to its story, unless the story is an Ask HN or another post with no page of its own; the page it links to is also its `discussionOf`, as with a Reddit post page's link. A discussion page's title is the story's, and its content is the post's own text followed by each comment, in page order (the thread's, each reply after the comment it replies to): its head (a comment block, see Item), with its depth (the row's `td.ind[indent]`), its author and the author's page (`.hnuser`), when it was posted (its `.age`'s title, `"2026-11-01T21:06:00 1793567160"`, read from the Unix seconds where given, else as UTC), its own page (`/item?id=<row id>`) and `op` if the story's submitter wrote it, then its paragraphs. Hacker News shows no score but on the user's own comments, so none is read. A deleted comment keeps its head, with `[deleted]` for its author and no text, as its replies are under it. Reddit listings (the front page or a subreddit, under any of its sorts: `/r/programming/new/`) link to each post's comments page, leaving out promoted posts and, on the front page of a logged-in user, posts Reddit recommends from subreddits the user has not joined (those without `is-subscribed`; a subreddit's own listing keeps all its posts) (the parser waits up to 10 s for a listing's posts to appear, as Reddit can be slow to fill its feed in a scrape's background tab). A listing reads about as many posts as it shows when opened in a tab of its own: the page holds only its first few, and the feed loads the rest from its `load-after` partial as its end nears the screen, which a background tab seldom does, so the parser fetches that partial's page itself, as the page would (with the user's session: Firefox's `content.fetch`), and each page after it, until the listing has 25 posts (at most 3 pages); pages the feed has already loaded are not fetched again. A post page links to what the post links to, unless it is a text post or something its content shows (a picture, a gallery, a video hosted by Reddit on `v.redd.it`); a post linking to a YouTube video still links to it. A post page also links, with weight 1, to its subreddit (`/r/IndieGaming/`, from the post's `subreddit-name`, old Reddit's `data-subreddit`, else its permalink), and a crosspost to its original's too, once if they are the same, so a search reaches what else is posted there; a post to a user's profile (`u_<name>`) links to no subreddit, its poster's page standing for it. A crosspost (a post whose `content-href`, or old Reddit's `data-url`, is another post's page) holds none of the post it embeds, only a card the page draws, so the parser fetches the original's page, as the page would, from the site being read, following a crosspost of a crosspost to the first, and reads what follows from the original: its pictures, its video, its text after any of the crosspost's own, and what it links to, which the crosspost then links to and is a `discussionOf`; the crosspost keeps its own title and comments, and its head names both authors, once if they are the same. If the original cannot be fetched, the crosspost is read as it stands. A post page's title is the post's, and its content is read as a Hacker News discussion's is: the post's head, which names its title, the page it links to (if it links to one), its authors, its score (`score`, old Reddit's `data-score`) and when it was posted (`created-timestamp`, old Reddit's tagline's `time[datetime]`), so the title heads the post in Read; then the post's own text, then each comment, modern or old Reddit, its head, then its text, each paragraph, list item and code block a block of its own (a modern comment's text is its `[slot="comment"]`, however deeply its markup nests it, but not a reply's). A modern comment's head takes its author, score and permalink from the `shreddit-comment`'s `author`, `score` and `permalink`, its depth from `depth` (else the comments it is nested in, as a fetched comments partial holds whole threads), and when it was posted from `created`, else its own `faceplate-timeago[ts]` or `time[datetime]`; an old Reddit comment's, from `data-author`, `data-permalink`, its tagline's `.score.unvoted` (the number in its title; none where the subreddit hides scores for now) and `time[datetime]`, its depth being the comments it is nested in. A comment's author links to `/user/<author>/` on the site it was read from, and is `op` when the post's (`shreddit-post[author]`, old Reddit's `data-author`). A scrape has 15 s in all, the page's loading included, so the parser waits for the post to appear (up to 5 s) rather than for a fixed delay, then for its comments (up to 2 s more) only if the post says it has any (`comment-count`; old Reddit's `data-comments-count`): a post without comments is read at once. Reddit sends a modern post page without its comments, which its `post-detail-modules` partial loads, and a background tab may not load it in time; so if no comments have appeared, the parser fetches that partial itself, as the page would, and, should it hold no comments, a comments partial within it. A post with a video hosted by Reddit (its `content-href`, or old Reddit's `data-url`, `https://v.redd.it/<id>`; for a crosspost whose original cannot be fetched, its player's, `shreddit-player[src]`) shows the video, with the player's `poster` frame: its block's video is the HLS playlist `https://v.redd.it/<id>/HLSPlaylist.m3u8`, which pairs each size of the video (`CMAF_480.mp4`, `CMAF_720.mp4`, …, silent) with its sound (`CMAF_AUDIO_128.mp4`) and needs no signature, unlike the player's own playlist and its packaged mp4s with sound, whose signatures expire within hours. A post linking to a YouTube video (its `content-href`, or old Reddit's `data-url`: `youtube.com/watch?v=<id>`, `youtu.be/<id>`, `/shorts/<id>`, `/live/<id>` or `/embed/<id>`, on `www.`, `m.` or `youtube-nocookie.com`), or, for a crosspost whose original cannot be fetched, embedding one (its `shreddit-embed`'s `html`, an `<iframe>` of `youtube.com/embed/<id>`), shows the video, its block's video the watch page `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>` and its poster `https://i.ytimg.com/vi/<id>/hqdefault.jpg`, before the post's own text, which may follow it on a post with a link. Old Reddit posts show their video, Reddit's or YouTube's, as modern ones do. A post linking to a RedGIFs GIF (`content-href` `https://www.redgifs.com/watch/<id>`) shows the GIF's video, not its pictures: the parser has the extension's background fetch the watch page, as RedGIFs lets no other site read its pages (its CORS rules), which the background, with its host permissions, is not held to: a scraper's `fetchText(url)` (`content-scripts/utils/dom.js`) sends the background a `fetch_text` message, which a scraper run as a content script (Firefox) can do, and one run as a user script (Chrome) once `userScripts.configureWorld({ messaging: true })` lets it, its messages arriving as `runtime.onUserScriptMessage`; the background fetches only `http(s)` URLs and takes its metadata's `video.contentUrl`, the copy with sound (`<Name>.mp4`) rather than the silent one it names (`<Name>-silent.mp4`), with `video.thumbnailUrl` as its poster; the post still links to the watch page. A Guardian page without an article headline (a front such as `/international`, or a section) links to each article on it once, in page order; articles are told by the date in their path (`/world/2026/nov/01/…`), and newsletter sign-up pages (`/info/2022/nov/14/football-daily-email-sign-up`), dated like articles, are left out. A Guardian article's title is its headline's `<h1>` (an opinion piece's headline block also holds its author's byline and picture, which are left out of it), and its content starts with its lead picture, unless that is a video, and keeps each picture of its body where it stands among the paragraphs, with its caption. A Guardian gallery (`/world/gallery/2026/oct/31/…`) has no article headline either: its title is its `<h1>` and its content the paragraphs introducing it, then each picture with its caption, leaving out the cover, which repeats one of the gallery's pictures uncaptioned. A Reddit post's content, after its head, starts with its pictures: an image post's (its `content-href`, or old Reddit's `data-url`, on `i.redd.it` or naming an image file) at full size and uncaptioned, or each of a gallery's slides (`gallery-carousel`; old Reddit's `.media-gallery`) at full size, with the caption its poster gave it (new Reddit's is in the alt text, `r/IndieGaming - The caption`, which repeats the title on a picture with none), the preview shown in the page and the blurred copy drawn behind each slide being left out. A link post (one whose link is to a page off Reddit and not a video it plays) starts with the preview Reddit shows of that page instead (the first picture in the post from `external-preview.redd.it`, `preview.redd.it` or `thumbs.redditmedia.com`, at the largest size its `srcset` offers, uncaptioned, but not the blurred copy behind it nor a picture in the post's text); old Reddit's thumbnails are too small to keep. A post whose link is to a picture does not link to it, the picture being in its content. A RedGIFs watch page (`/watch/rightwhitefoxhound`) is one GIF, picture or gallery, followed by a feed of others; its title is the first line of the poster's description, without the tags the page appends to it (`"#Amateur #Big Ass"`, the metadata's keywords), and its content its video or pictures, then the rest of the description. A post whose description is empty, or whose first line is only an address (an anonymous upload's can be the page of whoever the site credits it to), has no title, the parser saying so (`untitled`) so the page's own is not taken: that title, also the headline in the page's JSON-LD metadata, only lists the tags (`"Animation Anime Porn GIF by anonymous"`), so a Reddit post linking to the GIF names it in Results instead (see Result display). Its details are its tags and its poster; an upload of no one's, which the metadata credits to `anonymous`, has no poster, and no head naming one. A GIF is a video block whose poster is the metadata's `video.thumbnailUrl` and whose video is the copy with sound: the metadata's `video.contentUrl` names the silent copy (`<Name>-silent.mp4`), and `<Name>.mp4` exists for every GIF, even one without sound. A picture's block is the metadata's `image.contentUrl`, and a gallery's are its slides (`.GalleryGif-Image`), uncaptioned, as their alt text only lists tags. The page draws its GIF (`.GifPreview[data-feed-item-id=…]`) after loading, its box empty at first and then filled, and a gallery its first slide before the others, so the parser waits up to 10 s for the GIF, then for a gallery until no slide has come for a second; if the GIF never comes, the metadata's video or picture stands alone. A niche's page (`/niches/bigtits`) is a listing of the GIFs posted to it (`.GifPreview[data-feed-item-id]`, `/watch/<id>`, waited for up to 10 s), leaving out those the site promotes among them, each in a module of its own (`.FeedModule`). It links, with weight 1, to its uploader (`.video-detail-badges`' `/profile/` link) and its category (that row's other one-part link, `/amateur-porn`), and to the videos related to it (`.video-list`, those hidden until **Show more** is pressed included), the first most strongly, leaving out the video itself, which may be listed under another category. Its details are its category, author, minutes (the player's `data-duration`, rounded, at least 1), views (`"3.8K"` as 3800), quality (its tallest file, `"720p"`) and published date (the metadata's `uploadDate`). A Guardian football match page (`/football/match/2026/nov/02/newcastleunited-v-everton`) has no article headline: its title is the page's, and its content the competition and ground, the score (`"Newcastle 0 - 0 Everton"`), each of the match's stats on a line of its own (`"Possession: Newcastle 45% Everton 55%"`), then the lineups and substitutes under a heading each, a line per team (`"Newcastle: 21 L. Hornicek, 33 D. Burn (yellow card), …"`); the league table it shows is left out, and it links to the match's live blog or report where its header gives them. A football live blog's headline is in its match header, which only the page's scripts draw (the page's own `<h1>` is in a `<noscript>`), so a page with a live blog (`#liveblog-body`) and no headline takes its title from its `og:title` and is otherwise read as an article. An interactive (`/environment/ng-interactive/…`) without a headline, which its scripts draw, is read as its `og:title` and `og:description`; and a front never links to the page itself (a dated page links to itself to skip to its content). The news sites among the web's most visited each have a scraper built on `newsPage` in `content-scripts/utils/dom.js`, which reads a site's page one of two ways. An article (a page whose JSON-LD says it is one, a `NewsArticle`, `ReportageNewsArticle`, `BlogPosting`, `Review` and the like, or whose address says so where the scraper names it) reads as its title (the page's heading, else its JSON-LD `headline`), then a head with its authors and when it was published, as its JSON-LD gives them (`jsonLdArticle`: its `author`s that are people, not an organization such as the paper itself, each with their page, which the article links to with weight 1, and its `datePublished`), then the paragraphs, headings, list items and pictures of its body, each element matching the site's body selector in turn (one that is itself a paragraph is read as one), without what its skip selector names (ads, bylines, related links, players); a picture given twice in a row at two sizes, for two screens, is kept once. An article whose body is not found is left to the default parse. Any other page (a home page, a section, a topic) is a listing of each story it links to, once and in page order, told by its address matching the site's pattern for its stories (its query and fragment left out), weighted as a news listing's links are.
